What a class is

Four things, and only one of them is a recipe.

The first is the knife. How to hold it, how to keep it sharp, and how to cut for an hour without your shoulder hurting. Most home cooks are slow because their knife is dull and their board is in the wrong place.

The second is efficiency. Where things go on the counter, what goes in the pan first, and how to have four things finish at the same time. The third is playing. I want you comfortable enough to open the fridge, look at what is there, and start, which is a different skill from following instructions.

The fourth is cooking with what is available. That is the whole business. I have run The Spontaneous Cafe since 2009 and I write most menus the night before, from what a farm stand had that afternoon. You can learn to do the same thing on a Tuesday with half an onion and some greens.

Three formats

Pick the one that matches your day.

All three run at the same rates. Tell me the group and I will tell you which one fits.

Knife skills and kitchen efficiency

Half a day at a board with me watching your grip. We sharpen your own knives first, then work through the cuts you actually use at home. By the end you will have broken down a chicken and cut a case of vegetables, and your hands will know why the order matters.

Cook what you found

A full day that starts at the farmers market or in the forest and ends at your table. We buy or gather with no menu written, and then I teach you to build one out of what is in the bags. This is the class people book after a foraging day, and it is my favourite one to teach.

Interactive dinner party

Your group cooks the meal with me instead of waiting for it. Some people take a station and some sit with a drink and watch, which is how every party of this kind actually goes. Everyone eats at the same table at the end.

Before you book

Four questions I get every week.

How many people can take a class?

There is no cap. Two is a good class and so is twelve, and the format changes with the number. Small groups get more time on the board with me. Large groups work better as stations, where each pair takes one dish and I move between them.

Do we need any equipment?

A working stove, a sink and some counter space. I bring knives, boards and small equipment when a kitchen is short. If you want to learn on your own knives, leave them out and I will sharpen them at the start.

Can you work around dietary needs?

Yes, and I plan the class around them from the start. Substituting at the end never works as well. Tell me about allergies, vegetarian or vegan eaters and anything else when you book. Most of the way I cook is vegetable-led anyway, so this rarely costs anything.

Can I give a class as a gift?

Yes. People book these for birthdays and anniversaries, and the date can stay open until the person receiving it picks one. Call or email me and I will send you something you can hand over.
