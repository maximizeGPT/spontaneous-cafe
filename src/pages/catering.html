How it comes together

Four steps between your first email and the first plate.

Weddings run the longest version of this; a retreat dinner can run it in two weeks.

  1. First call

    You talk it through

    Date, venue, head count, budget, and what kind of eating you want people doing.

  2. Weeks out

    Tasting or menu call

    For weddings, a tasting for two to four of you. For most other events, a call and a written menu.

  3. The week of

    He sources it

    Farm stands, the market, a ranch inland, the boats at Noyo, and whatever the woods have that week. Final counts lock a few days before.

  4. The day

    The crew sets up and cooks

    They arrive early with the kitchen packed in the van, cook, serve, clear, and leave the venue the way they found it. Where there is a lawn and a power outlet instead of a kitchen, they prep off site and finish on arrival.

Service styles

Three ways to put the food in front of people.

The style changes the staff count, the rental list and the price more than the menu does.

Seated multi-course

Courses arrive in order to people in chairs. It is the slowest way to feed a room, and past about forty the staff count climbs.

Family style at long tables

Platters go down the middle and people pass them to each other, the style Matt cooks most often for weddings and reunions.

Grazing and passed bites

A standing table people pick at all evening, plus small things carried through the room. For the same budget it feeds more people than a seated dinner.

Good to know

The practical side.

Guest counts
Small gatherings run on the three hour tiers. Weddings and crewed events get a written quote instead.
Venues
Private homes, rentals, barns, ranches, gardens and rented halls. An unfamiliar venue gets a walk-through before the day.
Staff and rentals
Cooks and service staff scale to the head count and style, and the count sits in your quote. Tables, linen, china, glass and a field kitchen come from a rental company, arranged by Matt or by your planner.
Retreats
Multi-day retreats run on the same tiers as any event. See cooking classes for the interactive dinner format.
Dietary needs
Vegan, gluten free and allergy plates are designed with the menu on the first draft. Send the list with your final counts.
Travel
Greater Mendocino. Fort Bragg to Elk and inland to Anderson Valley is routine; beyond that, travel goes into the quote.

Questions

Four that come up on every first call.

How far ahead should I book, and what holds the date?

Summer and autumn wedding dates go early, so a year out is reasonable. A deposit holds the date, and the balance comes due close to the event.

Do you do tastings?

For weddings, yes: a shortened version of the menu for two to four people, a few months before the date. Smaller events are handled with a menu call.

What about drinks?

I do not sell or pour alcohol. Hire a licensed bartender if you want it poured. Tell me the menu and I will say what I would drink with it.

What happens if it rains?

It rains here, and the fog arrives whether or not it does. Every outdoor event gets a wet plan: a tent, a barn, or a room we move to, with the cutoff agreed in advance. The menu stays the same either way.
