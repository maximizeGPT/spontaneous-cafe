How it comes together

Four steps between your first email and the first plate.

Weddings run the longest version of this. A retreat dinner can run the whole thing in two weeks.

  1. First call

    You talk it through

    Date, venue, head count, budget, and what kind of eating you want people doing. Twenty minutes on the phone settles the shape of the thing.

  2. Weeks out

    Tasting or menu call

    For weddings Matt cooks a tasting for two to four of you. For most other events a call and a written menu does the job.

  3. The week of

    He sources it

    Farm stands, the market, a ranch inland, the boats at Noyo, and whatever is up in the woods that week. Final counts lock a few days before.

  4. The day

    The crew sets up and cooks

    They arrive early with the kitchen packed in the van, then cook, serve, clear, and leave the venue the way they were handed it.

Service styles

Three ways to put the food in front of people.

The style changes the staff count, the rental list and the price more than the menu does.

Seated multi-course

Courses arrive in order to people in chairs, plated in the kitchen and carried out together. It is the slowest way to feed a room. Past about forty the service staff count starts to climb.

Family style at long tables

Platters go down the middle and people pass them to each other. Nobody sits waiting for a plate, and the room is loud inside four minutes. This is the style Matt cooks most often for weddings and reunions.

Grazing and passed bites

A standing table people pick at all evening, plus small things carried through the room. Good for a rehearsal night or an offsite where everyone needs to keep moving and talking. For the same budget it feeds more people than a seated dinner.

Questions

Four that come up on every first call.

How far ahead should I book, and what holds the date?

Summer and autumn wedding dates go early, so a year out is reasonable. A deposit holds the date and the balance comes due close to the event.

Do you do tastings?

For weddings, yes. I cook a shortened version of the menu for two to four people, usually a few months before the date. Smaller events are handled with a menu call.

What about drinks?

I do not sell or pour alcohol. You buy what you want and hire a licensed bartender if you want it poured for you. Tell me the menu you have chosen and I will tell you what I would drink with it.

What happens if it rains?

It rains here, and the fog arrives whether or not it does. Every outdoor event I quote gets a wet plan written into it: a tent, a barn, or a room we move to. We agree the cutoff time in advance, and the menu stays the same either way.
