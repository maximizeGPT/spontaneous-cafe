What the day is

A walk with baskets, and a route I pick that morning.

I read the tide chart the night before and check what has come up since the last rain. Then I set the route. You carry a basket. I carry the knife, the bags and thirty years of knowing which ridge holds chanterelles in a dry November. Nothing goes in the basket unless I have identified it.

Couples drive up from San Francisco for a weekend and give me one day of it. Families book it for kids who want to hold something they picked. Small groups split one price four or six ways, which is how most friends do it. One couple spent a day of their engagement trip on the coast with me, harvested salt at low tide, filled two baskets with mushrooms, and ate all of it that night in their rental.

You do not need to know anything. Most people arrive able to name one wild plant and leave able to name fifteen.

The full day

Four stops, in this order, because the tide goes first.

Times move with the tide chart. The shape of the day holds.

  1. Low tide

    Salt off the rocks

    We collect seawater and pick sea vegetables out of the splash zone. The water boils down at home to about a cup of salt from five gallons. It goes in a jar and comes with you.

  2. Late morning

    Into the forest

    Mushrooms, greens and berries under the redwoods. I show you the edible one and the thing that looks exactly like it, side by side, before either goes near a basket.

  3. Midday

    Farm stands and the market

    Unstaffed stands on back roads where you leave cash in a box. We are usually the only car there. Whatever the stands are short on, we buy at the farmers market.

  4. Evening

    Dinner from the baskets

    I cook a multi-course meal in your kitchen from what we gathered. You sit down. Dinner is booked separately, and most full days end this way.

How the half day differs

A half day runs four to five hours and covers two of those four stops. When low tide falls in the morning we take the beach and the forest. When it falls late we start in the woods and finish at the farm stands. The half day still ends with a full basket, and you can add dinner to it the same way.

September to November

Autumn baskets

Forest

Chanterelles

Two weeks after the first real rain, on the same ridges every year. This is the busiest month I have.

Forest

Boletes, hedgehogs

Boletes get eaten by bugs from the inside, so I show you how to check the stem before you cut.

Forest

Black trumpets, late

Roadside

Huckleberries

Small, dark and slow. An hour of picking fills one quart, which is enough for a galette.

Farm

Varieties that never reach a supermarket, sold out of crates by the road.

Farm

Winter squash, quince

Forest bathing and nature excursions

We walk slowly and stop often, and I keep the talking to a minimum. I point out what is edible when you ask, though the day is built around being under the trees, and most baskets come home empty. People who book this usually arrive tired from a city.

Before you book

The five things people ask me.

What should I wear and bring?

Layers, and boots or shoes you are happy to get wet. The coast runs cold and foggy in summer and wet in winter, so bring a rain shell in any season. Water and a snack help on the full day. Leave the white jacket at the rental.

What happens if the weather turns?

We go out in rain and fog, and some of the best mushroom days are the worst weather days. If a storm makes the roads or the rocks unsafe I move the route inland or we move the date. Cancellation terms are worth confirming with me when you book.

Can we bring kids?

Yes, and families book this often. Children are better than adults at spotting things low to the ground. For younger kids I usually suggest the half day, and I keep the walking short and the beach in the plan.

Is it safe to eat what we find?

I have foraged for over thirty years, in Northern California and well outside it. I teach the lookalikes first, so you learn the dangerous one before the good one. Anything either of us is unsure about stays in the woods, and I check every basket again before I cook out of it.

How far do we drive?

We stay in the greater Mendocino area, and most days total under an hour of driving spread across several stops. Some of it is on back roads and gravel. Tell me where you are staying and I will build the route around it.
