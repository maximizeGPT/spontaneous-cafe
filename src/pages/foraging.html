What a foraging day includes

You walk with Matt, carry a basket, and learn to tell each plant and mushroom from the thing that looks like it. What you gather goes home with you, and the twelve-hour day ends with dinner cooked from it.

  • Salt from the shore, mushrooms and greens from the forest, produce from the farm stands
  • Everything identified as you go, before it goes in the basket
  • A multi-course dinner cooked from the baskets on the 12-hour day

The four services combine into one booking, most often a foraging morning and a private dinner that night. Build your own day.

The full day

Four stops

An example route, adapted to the season, weather and your group. Choose one stop for a shorter outing or combine several over a longer day.

  1. Low tide

    Salt off the rocks

    Explore the shoreline and learn how coastal ingredients find their way into Matt’s cooking. Shore visits depend on the tide, conditions and suitable access.

  2. Late morning

    Into the forest

    Mushrooms, greens and berries under the redwoods. Matt puts the edible one and the thing that looks exactly like it side by side, before either goes near a basket.

  3. Midday

    Farm stands and the market

    Unstaffed stands on back roads where you leave cash in a box. Yours is usually the only car there. Whatever the stands are short on comes from the farmers market.

  4. Evening

    Dinner from the baskets

    Matt cooks a multi-course meal in your kitchen out of what came home. You sit down. The twelve-hour day ends this way.

Questions people ask

What should I wear and bring?

Layers, and boots or shoes you are happy to get wet. The coast runs cold and foggy in summer and wet in winter, so bring a rain shell in any season. Water and a snack help on the long day. Leave the white jacket at the rental.

What happens if the weather turns?

The route depends on the forecast, tides and access. We’ll discuss alternatives if conditions affect your outing, and agree on any rescheduling or cancellation terms before you book.

Can we bring kids?

Yes, and families book this often. Children are better than adults at spotting things low to the ground. For younger kids I usually suggest three or six hours, and I keep the walking short and the beach in the plan.

Is it safe to eat what we find?

Identification is central to the day. We discuss edible species and their lookalikes, and anything uncertain stays where it is. The menu and route depend on what can be confidently identified and responsibly gathered.

How far do we drive?

We stay in the greater Mendocino area, and most days total under an hour of driving spread across several stops. Tell me where you are staying and I will build the route around it.
