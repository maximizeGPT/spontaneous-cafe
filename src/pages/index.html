A day with me

Two people came up for a foraging day. This is what happened.

A couple on their engagement trip booked the full day. The order below is the order we did it in.

  1. Morning, low tide

    Salt from the beach

    We walked down at low tide and harvested sea salt off the rocks. It went home with them in a jar and onto the table that night.

  2. Late morning

    Mushrooms on the ridge

    Into the woods with baskets. I show you what is edible, what is not, and how to tell them apart when they look alike.

  3. Midday

    Farm stands and the market

    Unmanned farm stands on back roads where you leave cash in a box. Then the farmers market for whatever the stands did not have.

  4. Evening

    Dinner in their rental

    I cooked a multi-course farm-to-table meal in their kitchen from everything we had gathered. They sat down. That was the day.

Where the food comes from

Small farms, honor boxes, the forest and the shore.

I buy from small local farms, a couple of larger ones, and farm stands so small nobody staffs them. You leave the money in a box. We are often the only people there. The rest I harvest myself: mushrooms and greens from the forest, salt and sea vegetables from the coast, huckleberries when they are on.

Menus follow what showed up that week. I write them the night before, sometimes the morning of. Whole foods, local, organic, and I can work with whatever a place has.

About

I'm Matt. I've lived on the North Coast since 2008.

I've been foraging for more than thirty years, in Northern California and a long way from it. I taught cooking for twenty-three of those, including at Living Light in Fort Bragg and at Esalen. In 2009 I started calling what I do The Spontaneous Cafe, because the menu is decided by what shows up.

Last year I cooked a free weekend for the town. Most weeks I'm cooking for people who drove up from San Francisco, or for their friends who heard about it.
