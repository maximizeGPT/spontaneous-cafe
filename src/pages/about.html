About Matt

I'm Matt. I forage and I cook.

Thirty years in the woods, twenty-three teaching people to cook, and since 2008 all of it on the North Coast.

The short version

The menu is decided by what shows up.

I came to the North Coast in 2008 and stayed. Foraging came long before that. I have been doing it for more than thirty years, in Northern California and a long way outside it, and I have put as much time into reading about it as I have into walking. Both halves matter when you are looking at two mushrooms that are easy to mistake for each other.

I have taught cooking for twenty-three years. Some of that was at Living Light Culinary Arts Institute in Fort Bragg, some of it at Esalen, and a lot of it in other people's kitchens with whatever they had on hand.

In 2009 I started calling the work The Spontaneous Cafe. The name is literal. I do not write a menu and then go shopping for it. I go out, I see what the tide and the weather and the farm stands have done that week, and the menu comes back with me.

How I cook

Six words I actually mean.

People use these words on menus without much behind them.

Whole foods
I cook the whole vegetable and the whole animal. Tops, stems, bones and trim go into stock, pickles, or the course after the one you are eating.
Farm to table
Most of what I serve was in the ground, on the rock or on the tree that morning. The drive from where it grew to your kitchen is usually under an hour.
Quality
Four ingredients that are right beat twelve that are close. If the fish off Noyo is mediocre that day, I change the course instead of serving it.
Local
Everything comes from the greater Mendocino area when it can. Citrus and stone fruit come from inland, because they will not ripen in the fog.
Organic
The farms I buy from grow without synthetic sprays, and I know the people who run them. Nobody sprays the forest or the shore at all.
Resourceful
I can put a dinner on the table in a vacation rental with two pans and a crowded fridge. Twenty-three years on, it is most of what I teach.

Sourcing

Small farms, honor boxes, the forest and the shore.

I buy from a handful of small farms and a couple of larger ones. The best of it comes from farm stands nobody staffs. You take what you want, you put cash in the box, and most mornings we are the only people there. What the stands miss, the farmers markets usually have.

The rest I go and get. Chanterelles, boletes and black trumpets from the ridge in their weeks. Nettles and miner's lettuce in spring. Huckleberries when they come on, which is a good afternoon and a stained shirt. Salt off the rocks at low tide, in a jar, on the table that night.

None of it is guaranteed.

Teaching

Twenty-three years of it.

I taught at Living Light Culinary Arts Institute in Fort Bragg. Raw and plant-based technique, knife work, and how to run a prep list so the day does not run you.

I have also taught at Esalen, down the coast in Big Sur, cooking for a house full of people and teaching while doing it. Both places attract students who want the reason behind the step, so that is how I teach now.
