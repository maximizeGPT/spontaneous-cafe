About Matt

I’m Matt. I forage and I cook.

Thirty years in the woods, twenty-three teaching people to cook, and since 2008 all of it on the North Coast.

The short version

The menu is decided by what shows up.

I came to the North Coast in 2008 and stayed. Foraging came long before that. I have been doing it for more than thirty years, in Northern California and a long way outside it, and I have put as much time into reading about it as I have into walking. Both halves matter when you are looking at two mushrooms that are easy to mistake for each other.

I have taught cooking for twenty-three years. Some of that was at Living Light Culinary Arts Institute in Fort Bragg, some of it at Esalen, and a lot of it in other people’s kitchens with whatever they had on hand.

In 2009 I started calling the work The Spontaneous Cafe. I go out, see what the tide, the weather and the farm stands have done that week, and the menu comes back with me.

How I cook

Whole foods, farm to table.

I cook whole foods. Vegetables come in whole and get used end to end, tops and stems included, in the dish or in a pickle for later. Meat and fish are bought, at the market and from the harbor. Farm to table here is a distance, usually under an hour from the ground to the kitchen, and most of what I serve was still growing that morning.

Quality is the part I will argue about. If the fish from Noyo is mediocre on the day, I change the course and keep the standard. I am also resourceful, which is the polite word for putting six courses on a vacation rental table with two pans and a crowded fridge.

Sourcing

Small farms, honor boxes, the forest and the shore.

I buy from a handful of small farms and a couple of larger ones. Everything is local when it can be, which on this coast means the greater Mendocino area; citrus and stone fruit come from inland because they will not ripen in the fog. The best of it comes from farm stands nobody staffs. You take what you want, you put cash in the box, and most mornings we are the only people there. What the stands miss, the farmers markets usually have.

The farms I buy from grow organic, without synthetic sprays, and I know the people who run them. Nobody sprays the forest or the shore at all. The rest I go and get. Chanterelles, boletes and black trumpets from the ridge in their weeks. Nettles and miner’s lettuce in spring. Huckleberries when they come on, which is a good afternoon and a stained shirt. Salt off the rocks at low tide, in a jar, on the table that night. Any of it can be missing on the day.

Teaching

Twenty-three years of it.

I taught at Living Light Culinary Arts Institute in Fort Bragg. Raw and plant-based technique, knife work, and how to run a prep list so the day does not run you.

I have also taught at Esalen, down the coast in Big Sur, cooking for a house full of people and teaching while doing it.

One weekend I cooked for the town of Mendocino for free. Most weeks the guests are people who drove up from San Francisco, or friends of theirs who heard about the food.
