How it works

You pick the date. Matt takes the rest of the day.

Five steps, the same every time, whether it is dinner for two or a full house.

  1. When you book

    You send the date and who is coming

    Matt needs the date, the head count, the address, and anything anyone cannot eat. That is enough to start.

  2. That morning

    He shops and forages

    Farm stands on the back roads, the market in town, and whatever is up in the woods that week. The menu is settled by the time the car is loaded.

  3. Late afternoon

    He cooks in your kitchen

    He arrives with his own knives and pans and sets up on your counter. Prep runs a few hours before the first course goes out.

  4. Evening

    You sit down

    Courses come out one at a time, plated in the kitchen and carried to the table. Nobody at the table gets up for anything.

  5. After

    He cleans up and leaves

    Dishes washed, counters wiped, the kitchen the way he found it. Leftovers stay with you in your own containers.

Who books it

Mostly people on a trip, and the people they tell afterwards.

Four kinds of booking come in more often than anything else.

Couples and families in rentals

Most of these dinners happen in vacation rentals on the Mendocino coast. Some are for two people at a small table. Some are for a house with three generations in it and a kitchen island covered in car keys.

Weekends up from San Francisco

People drive up Friday afternoon and want one night nobody has to plan, shop for or clean after. They book the dinner for the night the whole group is finally in the house.

Birthdays and anniversaries

A dinner at home instead of a restaurant reservation forty minutes down the coast. If there is a cake, a favorite dish or a bottle someone has been saving, say so and the menu gets built around it.

Referrals

A large share of the work comes from guests who ate one dinner and passed the number on. Rental owners and property managers send people too.

The menu

Written the night before, sometimes the morning of.

Until Matt has walked the farm stands he does not know what is out on the shelf, and until he has been up on the ridge he does not know whether the chanterelles came. So the menu gets written last, after the shopping. The business took its name from that.

Whole foods, local, organic where he can get it. Dietary needs and allergies go into the plan at the start, so send them with the booking. Vegan, gluten free, no dairy, a nine year old who eats nothing green. All of it is workable with notice.

Matt on rental kitchens: "I have cooked six courses out of a kitchen with two working burners and one dull knife." Whatever the kitchen is missing, he brings.

Good to know

The six things people ask before they book.

Where
Vacation rentals, second homes and full-time homes in the greater Mendocino area. Further out, ask, and Matt will say whether the drive works.
Group size
Two people up to a full house, with no fixed cap. Past about twelve he brings a second pair of hands.
Kitchen needs
A working stove, a sink, and some counter space. An oven helps and is not required. Two burners have been enough before now.
Dietary needs
Vegan, vegetarian, gluten free, dairy free, allergies, and picky kids. Send the list with the booking and it shapes the menu from the first draft.
Timing
Summer and holiday weekends fill first, so allow a couple of weeks. Midweek dates often work on short notice.
What he brings
Knives, pans, and whatever the kitchen turns out to be missing. Serving pieces if you need them. All the food.

Questions

Four Matt gets most weeks.

Do you shop, or do I?

I shop and I forage. You never go to a store. Ingredients come back to you at cost with the receipts, on top of the tier you booked.

Will you cook for only two people?

Yes, and plenty of my dinners are for two. Three hours covers a two-course dinner for a small table.

Do you serve the courses, or only cook them?

I plate in the kitchen, carry each course to the table, and clear it. For a larger group I bring someone to help with service so the food keeps moving.

Can you cook across several days?

Yes. Houses book a tier a day for a weekend or a week, and I plan the menus so nothing repeats.
